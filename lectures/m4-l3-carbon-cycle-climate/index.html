13Big idea 1 · Carbon cycle
Big idea 1 · carbon circulates through burning and storage

Carbon and CO₂ in the Earth system

The carbon cycle at a glance. Numbers are billions of metric tons of carbon. Adapted from IPCC (2022), in Sherman & Montgomery (2023), Ch. 11.

  1. Carbon has always been part of the Earth system, and of humanity: it is in our food, our DNA, the air and our fuels.
  2. Carbon moves naturally between the ocean, the atmosphere and the biosphere.
  3. More than 99% is locked in solid and liquid form, in rocks, sediments and the deep ocean.
  4. Burning fossil fuels adds CO₂ to the air faster than nature can store it away again.

Each bar is a carbon stock from the figure, in billions of tons. Every step to the right is 10 times bigger (K = thousand, M = million).

Warm-up answer: D

Rocks and sediments hold about 100,000 times more carbon than the air. They give it up only over millions of years.

The human shortcut

Coal, oil and gas took millions of years to form. We are moving that carbon into the air in a few hundred years.

13Big idea 2 · Natural change
Big idea 2 · Earth's climate has changed before

Natural causes can change the climate too

An asteroid hits the Yucatán Peninsula about 66 million years ago. Illustration: Science Photo Library/Alamy Stock Photo.

Asteroid impacts

Dust and gases from the Chicxulub impact blocked sunlight and changed the climate. Most species, including the large dinosaurs, died out.

Volcanoes

Large eruptions put out ash that cools Earth for a few years, and CO₂ over very long times.

The sun and Earth's orbit

Slow changes in Earth's orbit brought the ice ages and the warm periods between them.

So how do we know?These causes act over years or over many thousands of years. Today's warming is fast, and the evidence points to us.

13Big idea 2 · Climate science
Big idea 2 · natural causes or human causes?

Climate change science

Image courtesy of the Koshland Science Museum, National Academy of Sciences.

Natural causes
  • The sun's energy
  • Volcanic eruptions
  • Slow changes in Earth's orbit

None of these explain the fast warming since 1900.

Human causes
  • Industrial metabolism: burning coal, oil and gas for energy and goods
  • Land change: clearing forests and farming
"Most of the recent warming can be attributed to fossil fuel burning and other human activities that release carbon dioxide and other heat-trapping greenhouse gases into the atmosphere."The scientific consensus · America's Climate Choices: Advancing the Science of Climate Change, National Research Council (2010)

13Big idea 3 · Adaptation
Big idea 3 · sea level rise on the coast

Coastal impacts and adaptation strategies

Adaptation means adjusting ecological, social or economic systems in response to actual or expected climate change and its effects.

Impacts

  1. Some low-lying islands disappear.
  2. Coastal land floods and goes under water.
  3. More erosion and loss of coastal habitat.
  4. Salt water seeps into rivers and groundwater.

13Big idea 3 · Glacial lakes
Big idea 3 · melting glaciers fill mountain lakes

Glacial lakes are growing

Glacial lakes around the world. Source: Shugar et al. (2020), Nature Climate Change.

As glaciers melt, meltwater collects behind loose walls of rock and ice called moraines.

GLOF

A glacial lake outburst flood happens when that wall gives way and the lake empties all at once, sending water, mud and boulders down the valley.

13Big idea 3 · Solutions
Big idea 3 · mitigation, adaptation and climate justice

What can we all do?

  1. Our consumption and economy depend heavily on carbon-based energy. That decides how much CO₂ we put into the air and how much we disrupt its natural cycle.
  2. Consider changes in our own decisions about housing, mobility and consumption, and choose efficient, sustainable options.
  3. Be mindful of climate justice: the people and countries that contributed least to greenhouse gas emissions bear the heaviest impacts.

The stabilization triangle: the cuts needed to hold emissions flat instead of letting them keep growing. After Pacala and Socolow (2004).

MitigationCut the cause: clean energy, efficient homes, transit.
AdaptationLive with the change: shade, cooling centers, flood plans.

The takeaway

We are moving carbon from rock to air faster than nature can put it back.

  1. CO₂ in the air rises as long as emissions are bigger than what ocean and land absorb.
  2. Tree rings, ice cores and the CO₂ record show the recent warming comes from people.
  3. Mitigation cuts the cause; adaptation helps people live with the change.
