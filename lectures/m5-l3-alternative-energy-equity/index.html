16Big idea 1 · Alternatives
Big idea 1 · five ways to cut fossil-fuel use

What counts as alternative energy?

Energy efficiencyThe same service from less energy.
Renewable energyPower from sources that are not used up, such as wind, solar and geothermal.
Combined heat and power (CHP)Uses the waste heat from making electricity for heating or cooling.
Demand responseCuts use at peak times, so fewer costly plants must run.
Clean distributed generationSmall renewable or CHP systems at the place where the energy is used.

Why do it? Three kinds of benefit

GridA cheaper, steadier grid: fewer new plants and outages.
Emissions and healthLess air pollution and fewer greenhouse gases.
EconomySavings, jobs and money spent locally.

Quick question: a rooftop solar system on a house. Which kind is it?

Tap to check.

Two at once. It is renewable energy, and because it sits where the power is used, it is also clean distributed generation.

Source: US EPA (2018), sections 1.1 and 1.2 (Figure I-2).

2.3¢
Making a kilowatt-hourNew power plants and other new resources

The second bar is worked out from the EPA's statement that efficiency costs "about one-half to one-third" as much as new resources. It is not a price quoted in the reading.

Warm-up answerB. Saving energy costs about one-half to one-third as much as new supply.
Why it mattersEvery kilowatt-hour you save is one that no power plant has to make, burn fuel for, or pollute for.

Quick question: name one efficiency step that a school or home could take.

Tap for ideas.

Swap old bulbs for LEDs, seal and insulate buildings, or use appliances that need less power for the same job.

Source: US EPA (2018), section 1.2.1, citing an analysis of 20 state efficiency programs (LBNL, 2015; Lazard, 2017).

16Big idea 1 · Alternatives
Big idea 1 · the energy transition at one address

Make it, store it, use it

1 · MakeRooftop solar is clean distributed generation: it makes power where it is used.
2 · StoreA battery can hold extra renewable power for later and ease strain on the grid at peak times.
3 · UseEfficient appliances, and an electric car in place of a gas car, move a household away from fossil fuels.

Decarbonization means phasing out carbon emissions. The energy transition is the shift to clean energy that gets us there.

Sources: US EPA (2018), sections 1.1 and 1.2.1 (distributed generation, storage). The electric-car point is general knowledge, not from the reading.

16Big idea 3 · Energy justice
Case study · Bangladesh

SOLshare Grid in Bangladesh

A peer-to-peer microgrid energy exchange network powering millions of households

ChallengeBangladesh already had 3 million solar home systems, but they were off-grid and had no backup.
The ideaHouseholds trade access to electricity and earn income from their surplus, which could achieve up to 30% energy efficiency.
120 SOLgrids80,000+ people benefitted, 3,000+ MWh of energy generated each year, and about 4 million e-rickshaws or easy bikes.
Two toolsThe SOLbox is a bidirectional DC electricity meter. The SOLapp tracks energy use and payment details.
A global leaderBangladesh is now a leader in solar home system installations: 6 million households with 25 million people.

Big idea 3 · tap a card to see an example

What is energy justice?

Working definition

Energy justice means the benefits and the burdens of the energy system are shared fairly, and everyone has a voice in the decisions.

It applies the course idea of environmental justice: no group should carry an unfair share of pollution or risk.

Not in the EPA reading. This is a teaching framework built on the course glossary term Environmental Justice. The examples are illustrations, not data.

16Big idea 3 · Energy justice
Big idea 3 · winners, losers and a fair plan

A just transition

The reading says people gain
  • Households and businesses with lower energy costs
  • Workers in construction, manufacturing and services
  • Communities with cleaner air
The reading also says some lose
  • Income, jobs and output can fall for fossil-fuel producers and their suppliers
  • Utilities can lose revenue when customers use less electricity

Quick question: what would a fair plan do for a coal worker whose plant closes?

Tap to check.

Ideas: pay for retraining, help people move into the new clean-energy jobs, protect pensions, and bring workers and their town into the planning. These are examples to discuss, not policy from the EPA reading.

L16Alternative energy and energy equity
Energy transition

Clean energy pays back in many ways. A fair transition makes sure everyone shares the gains.

  1. Using less energy is the cheapest alternative, and renewables, storage and local generation can do the rest.
  2. Count all the benefits: a steadier grid, healthier air, and savings and jobs.
  3. Ask who is served, who has a say, and who bears the costs, including workers.
