Before you start · Phoenix metro area

Trees and the urban heat island

1 · The “Equality” trap

Uniform allocation: the same dollars everywhere

Every neighborhood gets an equal share. It sounds fair, but Arcadia already has shade and South Phoenix has almost none, so the hottest block is still the hottest.

2 · The “Equity” pivot

Needs-based allocation: more where the need is greatest

Put more of the money into the hottest, least-shaded blocks. The same budget lowers the heat where people are most at risk. You will test both in this exercise.

Linkages · how it connects

Shade is more than trees

Shade and health. Fewer trees mean hotter blocks, and more heat illness. People with no air conditioning are the most exposed.

Heat and money. Cooling bills hit low-income homes hardest. For a business, heat means lost work days, health costs and a risk to reputation.

Voice and power. Neighborhoods with less say at city hall are often last in line for trees and parks. Remember the voting rights timeline from the lecture.
