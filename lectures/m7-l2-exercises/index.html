21Part II · The SET frame
Part II · groups of 4 · about 8 minutes

Map your list onto a SET system

Social (S)People, norms and money: willingness to pay, status, schools, road rage, affordability.
Technological (T)Built things: light rail, Loop 202, zoning codes, air-conditioning load, EV or gas cars.
Ecological (E)Nature and resources: tailpipe CO2 and smog, Salt River and CAP water, desert habitat, Tempe heat island.

Two pairs join to make a group of 4. Draw three circles. Put at least two items from your table in each, and label at least two arrows.

21Part III · Three biases
Part III · behavioral economics debrief

Three biases behind the choice

1 · Present bias and salience

Sticker shock today

Why fixate on $1,650 versus $1,850 now, while underestimating about $700 a month in car costs and 350+ hours in traffic?

2 · Split incentives and externalities

Who pays?

When one resident picks a private yard, who pays for the air pollution, freeway congestion and water use?

3 · Choice architecture

Redesign the choice

How could a developer, employer or city planner make the sustainable choice the easiest financial choice?

21Part III · Redesign
Part III · choice architecture · individual or group

Make the sustainable choice the easy one

Your task

Pick one actor: a real estate developer, an employer or a city planner. Change a Technological (T) or Social (S) incentive so the sustainable choice costs less than the other one.

Ideas to start from

Employer transit passes. Denser, walkable town centers in Queen Creek. Shaded walking and bike corridors in Tempe.

21Class poll
Class poll · one answer

Now that you have seen the full monthly cost, where would you live?

21Takeaway
Closing takeaway
Every housing choice is private, and every housing choice is also a vote on roads, rail, water and heat. When the sustainable choice is the easy, cheap one, people make it without being told to.
