    The big idea · why sustain Earth?

    There is no other place like Earth

    Earth

    Air
    21% oxygen; a thick blanket of air
    Water
    Liquid oceans, rivers and rain
    Temperature
    About 59 °F on average
    Shield
    Ozone layer and magnetic field block harmful radiation
    vs

    Mars

    Air
    Less than 1% of Earth's pressure, about 95% CO₂
    Water
    Frozen in polar ice and underground
    Temperature
    About −80 °F on average
    Shield
    No ozone layer and no global magnetic field

    Some propose making Mars habitable. A NASA-funded study (2018) found Mars does not have enough carbon dioxide to warm it, even using all of it. Think how much energy that would take. The better idea is to sustain the planet we have.

    The simple reason to sustain Earth: nowhere else has air, water and temperature that are just right for life. Ask: what would it take to give Mars breathable air, liquid water and a shield from radiation? The 2018 study is Jakosky and Edwards, "Inventory of CO₂ available for terraforming Mars", Nature Astronomy 2, funded in part by NASA. They concluded terraforming is not possible with present-day technology. Mars figures are NASA fact-sheet averages, rounded.

    10Big idea 1 · Processes
    Big idea 1 · atmospheric processes

    A thin layer doing big jobs

    Thermosphere~85–600 km
    Mesosphere~50–85 km
    Stratosphere~12–50 km · ozone layer
    Troposphere0–~12 km · all our weather

    Dry air by volume

    Nitrogen 78% · Oxygen 21% · Argon 0.9% · Carbon dioxide ~0.04%

    How it supports life

    • Provides oxygen for breathing and CO₂ for photosynthesis
    • Protects us from harmful solar radiation: stratospheric ozone blocks most UV
    • Regulates temperature: greenhouse gases keep Earth near 59 °F instead of about 0 °F
    • Drives the water cycle, moving heat and water around the planet
    Layer heights vary with latitude and season, so they are rounded. Without greenhouse gases Earth's average would be about −18 °C (0 °F) instead of about 15 °C (59 °F). Link back to Module 3's life-support systems: air, water and land.

    10Big idea 1 · Weather
    Same air, two time scales

    Weather is what you get. Climate is what you expect.

    Weather

    Hours to days

    Short-term changes in temperature, moisture and wind at one place.

    "It's 115 °F in Tempe this afternoon."

    Climate

    30-year average

    The long-term average of weather conditions for a region.

    "Phoenix Julys average highs above 100 °F."

    Heat index

    What it feels like

    Combines air temperature and relative humidity in the shade into a "feels like" temperature.

    Temperature varies across Earth's surface mostly because each latitude receives a different amount of solar radiation.

    Quick check: is one record-cold week proof that climate isn't warming? Give pairs 60 seconds. The heat index matters during Phoenix's humid monsoon, when 105 °F can feel much hotter.

    Good ozone, bad ozone

    10Big idea 1 · Pollutants
    Game · primary or secondary?

    Sort the pollutants

    Primary: harmful in the form it is emitted. Secondary: formed in the air when primary pollutants react.

    Carbon monoxide from car exhaust
    Ground-level ozone
    Sulfur dioxide from a coal plant
    Sulfuric acid in rain
    Benzene and other VOCs from gasoline
    Photochemical smog
    Dust kicked up by a haboob
    Lead and asbestos
    Examples follow the lecture notes: primary pollutants include CO, pesticides, benzene, asbestos, lead and VOCs; secondary pollutants include smog, ground-level ozone and nitrogen dioxide. Wind can carry dust and small particles great distances, which is why dust counts as a primary pollutant even far from its source.

    10Solutions
    Sustainability solutions · Maricopa County Air Quality

    What can we do about bad ozone?

    Demand side · what people choose
    • Transportation: carpool, transit, bike, walk, telework
    • Be idle-free; avoid long drive-thru lines and rush-hour traffic
    • Reduce wood burning; refuel after dark
    • Skip gas leaf blowers and off-road vehicles on ozone days
    Supply side · rules for producers
    • Industrial emissions: caps, permits, surface-coating rules
    • Product and solvent controls; cleaner-burning gasoline
    • Energy efficiency and renewable power
    • Commercial fleets and equipment: idling limits, energy transition

    County campaign: Help Keep Ozone Away, Commit to One Day! Make cleaner air choices at least one day a week.

    Ask first: what are the things you could be doing to reduce bad ozone? Collect ideas, then reveal the two lists. Demand-side actions change how much pollution we ask for; supply-side actions change how cleanly things are produced. The next slide lets students try both. Source: maricopa.gov/5914/Outreach and the Commit to One Day campaign page.

    10Urban heat island
    Phoenix · heat made by the city itself

    The urban heat island effect

    • Phoenix's temperature is compounded by the heat island.
    • Concrete and asphalt absorb and trap heat all day and release it at night.
    • High temperatures harm human health and can be deadly.
    • Impacts are worse for vulnerable and minority groups: people outdoors, older adults, homes without working AC.
    +9 °Frise in Phoenix nighttime temperatures, 1948–2000, as the city grew
    602heat deaths confirmed in Maricopa County in 2024 (645 in 2023)
    70%of AC units in indoor heat deaths (2024) were not working
    10% → 25%Phoenix tree canopy today and its 2030 goal

    Sources: ASU and City of Phoenix urban forest study; Maricopa County 2024 heat deaths report.

    The heat island is strongest at night: pavement and buildings release stored heat, so the city never cools down and bodies never recover. That is why overnight lows matter as much as daytime highs. The 2024 figure is the county's March 2025 count of 602 confirmed deaths. Among indoor deaths where an AC unit was present, 70% of the units were not working.

    Demand is how much cooling and power people use; supply is the shade, cool surfaces, power and help that the city and utility provide. At the start, evening power demand is above supply, so the block faces rolling outages. Households can fix the grid alone but not the dangerous nights; the city can cool streets and help vulnerable people but cannot afford everything.     Big idea 2 · adapted from Sherman & Montgomery (2023)

    A cool invention that thinned the shield

    Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) were cheap, safe to handle and didn't burn. We used them everywhere:

    Cooling

    Refrigeration and air conditioning

    Sprays

    Propellants in medical and household aerosols

    Foams

    Blowing agents for foam packaging and insulation

    Cleaning

    Solvents and cleaning agents

    CFCs drift upto the stratosphere
    →
    UV frees chlorineone atom destroys many O₃
    →
    Thinner ozone layermore UV-B reaches the ground
    →
    Skin cancer, cataractscrop and ocean damage
    The ozone layer absorbs nearly all UV-C and most UV-B. When it thins, more UV-B reaches the surface. Illustration in the original slides: theozonehole.com/cfc.htm. Ask: CFCs were a real invention that solved problems; why couldn't anyone see the harm at first? (Stable molecules, delayed effects, far away in the stratosphere.)
    10Big idea 2 · Solutions
    Big idea 2 · finding sustainable solutions

    Fixing the ozone layer

    Phase out

    Stop producing CFCs and other ozone-destroying chemicals.

    Innovate

    Alternative chemicals, backed by economic incentives.

    Cooperate

    Global agreement: signed in 1987, in force in 1989.

    1974

    Molina and Rowland warn that CFCs can destroy ozone

    1985

    Scientists report the Antarctic ozone hole

    1987

    Montreal Protocol agrees to phase out CFCs

    2009

    First UN treaty ratified by every country

    2016

    Kigali Amendment phases down HFCs

    ~2066

    Antarctic ozone projected back to 1980 levels

    Connect to Module 1: this is collective action on a global commons. Why did it work here when climate agreements have been harder? Few producers, substitutes were available, and the harm was visible. Kigali targets HFCs, the CFC replacements, because they are strong greenhouse gases.

    10Big idea 3 · Solutions
    Big idea 3 · reducing the threat of acid rain

    Cap the pollution, trade the permits

    In 1990 the Clean Air Act Amendments created a tradeable emission allowance program. The plan aimed to cut SO₂ emissions by 10 million tons by 2000. By 2010, SO₂ emissions were well below the cap, about half of their earlier level.

    Strengths
    • A voluntary market with incentives
    • Rewards innovation and cost-effective cuts
    • Moderate, predictable change for industry
    Weaknesses
    • No change in pollution behaviour is required of any one plant
    • Leaves systemic problems in place
    • Pollution can stay concentrated near plants that buy permits

    Debate in pairs: is it fair to let a company pay to keep polluting if the total goes down?

    Plants got a fixed number of allowances; a plant that cut SO₂ cheaply could sell its spare allowances to one that couldn't. Link to the ozone game: a cap is a supply-side tool. The last weakness is an environmental justice question, which ties back to Module 1. Source for the policy history: Layzer (2016).

    The takeaway

    Our socio-economic factors disrupt natural cycles and processes, but we should also be responsible for the solutions.

    1. The atmosphere is Earth's life support; human activity changes its composition and heat.
    2. The Montreal Protocol shows global cooperation and innovation can repair the ozone layer.
    3. Caps and trading cut acid rain; solutions work best on both the demand and supply side.
