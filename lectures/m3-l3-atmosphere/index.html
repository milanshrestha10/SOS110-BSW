    3.3Life-support system
    Why air is a life-support system

    A thin layer doing big jobs

    Thermosphere~85–600 km
    Mesosphere~50–85 km
    Stratosphere~12–50 km · ozone layer
    Troposphere0–~12 km · all our weather

    Dry air by volume

    Nitrogen 78% · Oxygen 21% · Argon 0.9% · Carbon dioxide ~0.04%

    • Oxygen for breathing, CO₂ for photosynthesis
    • Stratospheric ozone blocks most harmful UV light
    • Greenhouse gases keep Earth near 15 °C on average instead of about −18 °C
    • Moves heat and water around the planet
    Layer heights vary with latitude and season, so they are rounded. Link back to the Module 3 idea of life-support systems: air, water and land.
    3.3Weather vs climate
    Same air, two time scales

    Weather is what you get. Climate is what you expect.

    Weather

    Hours to days

    The state of the atmosphere at a place and time: temperature, rain, wind, humidity.

    "It's 115 °F in Tempe this afternoon."

    Climate

    Decades

    The long-term average and range of weather in a region, usually over 30 years.

    "Phoenix Julys average highs above 100 °F."

    Quick check: is one record-cold week proof that climate isn't warming? Talk to a neighbour for 60 seconds.

    3.3Ground-level ozone
    A secondary pollutant

    How ground-level ozone forms

    NOₓengines, power plants
    +
    VOCsfuel vapour, solvents, plants
    +
    ☀ Sunlight + heatlong, hot afternoons
    →
    O₃ground-level ozone
    Good up high

    In the stratosphere, ozone absorbs ultraviolet light before it reaches us.

    Bad nearby

    At ground level the same molecule irritates lungs, worsens asthma and damages crops.

    Return to the warm-up poll: Phoenix's hot, sunny, still summer afternoons are ideal for ozone formation. The US health standard is 70 ppb averaged over 8 hours.

    3.3Stratospheric ozone
    A policy success story

    Fixing the ozone layer

    Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) from spray cans, fridges and air conditioners drift up to the stratosphere, where UV light frees chlorine that destroys ozone.

    1974

    Molina and Rowland warn that CFCs can destroy ozone

    1985

    Scientists report the Antarctic ozone hole

    1987

    Montreal Protocol agrees to phase out CFCs

    2009

    First UN treaty ratified by every country

    2016

    Kigali Amendment phases down HFCs

    ~2066

    Antarctic ozone projected back to 1980 levels

    Connect to Module 1: this is collective action on a global commons. Why did it work here when climate agreements have been harder? (Few producers, available substitutes, visible harm.)
    3.3Acid rain
    Another secondary pollutant

    How acid rain forms

    SO₂ + NOₓcoal plants, vehicles
    +
    Water vapourin clouds
    →
    H₂SO₄ + HNO₃sulfuric and nitric acid
    →
    Rain below pH 5.6
    Water

    Acidified lakes lose fish and amphibians.

    Land

    Leaches nutrients from forest soils and damages trees.

    Policy

    The 1990 Clean Air Act Amendments capped SO₂ from power plants with tradable allowances.

      Three things to remember
      1. The atmosphere is a life-support system: it gives us oxygen, blocks UV and keeps Earth warm enough for life.
      2. Primary pollutants come straight from sources. Secondary ones, like ground-level ozone and acid rain, form in the air.
      3. Policy has fixed air problems before: the Montreal Protocol and the SO₂ cap show collective action works.
