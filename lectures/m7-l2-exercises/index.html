Scenario

The scenario

Rent in Downtown Tempe

Your pay is about $3,900 a month after taxes. A common rule of thumb keeps housing and transportation together near $1,650 to $1,750.

A walkable, high-energy district. You can reach work, food and friends on foot, by bike or by light rail.

Own in Queen Creek

Same pay, same rule of thumb. You buy a spacious house with a yard and garage in a quiet, family-oriented area with newer schools.

Almost every trip, including the 30 to 45+ minute commute, is by car.

Teaching numbers from your instructor. Owning also brings a down payment, property taxes and repairs, which are not priced here.

Systems linkages

How where you live reaches the land and the air

Technological (T): what is built

Tempe: light rail, Orbit buses, walkable streets, apartments that share walls.

Queen Creek: Loop 202 and US-60, single-family zoning, a large house to cool, gas cars.

Ecological (E): what nature takes

Tempe: a lower carbon footprint per person, but a strong urban heat island.

Queen Creek: tailpipe CO2 and smog, Salt River and CAP water for lawns, desert habitat split by sprawl.

Social to technological (S → T). Many people choosing big lots creates demand for wider freeways and single-family zoning.

Technological to social (T → S). With no light rail, every resident must drive, so congestion and road rage grow.

Social + technological to ecological (S + T → E). Car commuting and large-lot cooling raise greenhouse gases and water stress.

Closing takeaway
Where you live is a private choice with public effects. Every option gives up something, and the roads, rail, water and heat around us are shaped by thousands of choices like yours.
